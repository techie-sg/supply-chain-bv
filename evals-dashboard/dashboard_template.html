How to read these charts
  • Bars, x-axis: each group is one setup that was tested (chunk size, model, or dimensions, depending on the Compare menu). The small label under it says whether task adapters were on ("adapt.") or off ("plain").
  • Bars inside a group: how many results retrieval was allowed to return — top-1, top-3, top-5, or whatever fits in a 4,000-character budget. Bars usually rise left to right because more results give more chances to hit.
  • Hit chart, y-axis: % of the 63 scored questions where at least one correct playbook section appeared in the results. Example: 82.5% = 52 of 63 questions.
  • Recall chart, y-axis: for each question, the share of its expected sections that were found, averaged over all questions. It is stricter than hit, so it is always lower. A question expecting 3 sections that finds 2 counts as 67%.
  • Heatmap: the same percentages as a grid; darker = higher, outlined = best in that column.
  • One question moves a score by about 1.6 points, so gaps under ~5 points are noise.

Why jina-embeddings-v5-text-nano

Compared with v5-small, v4 and v3 (sections chunking, task adapters on, full float vectors).

  1. Best top-3 and top-5 hit rate. hit@3 0.825 and hit@5 0.873 are the highest of the four models. The app sends the top 3 sections to the LLM, so this is the metric closest to production.
  2. v4's leads are inside the noise. v4 is ahead on hit@1 (+0.048), MRR (+0.014) and recall@budget (+0.028), but a gap under ~0.05 is about three questions out of 63, and v4 is behind on hit@3 (−0.031) and hit@5. It is not a reliable win.
  3. Smallest vectors. 768 dims against 1024 (small, v3) and 2048 (v4), so v4 stores and searches 2.7× the numbers per chunk and is a 3.8B-parameter model, for no consistent gain.
  4. No reason to move up within v5. v5-small scores below nano on every metric in the table above.
  5. v3 is clearly worse (hit@3 0.683, MRR 0.596).
  6. The real lever is task adapters, not the model. They lift nano's hit@3 from 0.683 to 0.825 at no cost, which is a bigger move than any model swap.

Caveats: without adapters nano's hit@3 (0.683) is below v4 and v5-small, so this recommendation depends on turning adapters on and re-ingesting. With 63 questions the model ranking is close; recheck if the corpus or question set changes.

Findings

  1. Task adapters are the clearest win, at no cost — every chunking and model improves (v5-nano + sections: hit@3 0.683 → 0.825, MRR 0.547 → 0.664).
  2. A bigger model doesn't pay off — with adapters, v5-small and v4 are within noise of v5-nano; v3 is worst.
  3. Keep section chunking — fixed chunks only look better per k; at equal 4,000-char context, sections + adapters has recall 0.771 vs 0.651 for fixed-1600-0.
  4. Keep full-size float vectors — quality drops steeply below 512 dims and with binary vectors.
  5. The PDF switch cost ~5 points vs the Markdown corpus baseline (hit@1 0.46 → 0.40).
