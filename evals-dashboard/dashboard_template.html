DispatchDesk retrieval evals

Four factors, two metrics. Factors: embedding model, chunk size, number of chunks retrieved (k), chunking approach. Metrics: recall@k and precision@k. Retrieval only (no LLM), 63 scored questions, Jina task adapters on, PDF corpus (7 docs), full float vectors. One question moves recall by ~0.016; treat gaps under ~0.05 as noise.

recall@k = share of a question's expected playbook sections found in the top k chunks, averaged over questions. precision@k = share of the top k chunks that contain at least one expected section. A fixed-size chunk counts for every section it overlaps by ≥100 characters.

Why jina-embeddings-v5-text-nano and section chunking

Embedding model: nano is the smallest model that is as good as the rest

  • On sections, nano, v4 and v5-small are within noise of each other at every k (recall@3 0.676 / 0.663 / 0.644, precision@3 0.423 / 0.413 / 0.407; recall@5 0.765 / 0.775 / 0.755). Nano is ahead at k=3, v4 is ahead by 0.010 at k=5, both well under the ~0.05 noise band.
  • v3 is clearly behind (recall@3 0.595, precision@3 0.376).
  • Nano stores 768 dimensions per chunk against 1024 (v5-small, v3) and 2048 (v4, a 3.8B-parameter model), so a bigger model buys no measurable retrieval gain for more cost. This is a “no reason to pay more” argument, not a claim that nano is the most accurate.

Chunking: sections keep one topic per chunk, but this metric pair alone does not crown them

  • Against fixed chunks that send a similar amount of text (fixed-400, fixed-800), sections have the best recall@3 (0.676 vs 0.599 and 0.610) and recall@5 (0.765 vs 0.684 and 0.733). fixed-400 has higher precision@3 (0.503 vs 0.423), but it sends about 1.3k characters at k=3 and finds fewer of the expected sections.
  • Larger fixed chunks (fixed-1600, fixed-3200) score higher on both recall@k and precision@k. That is partly an artifact: each of their chunks covers several sections, so one slot is credited with several, and at k=3 they send about 3.9k and 6.4k characters against about 1.9k for sections. Recall@k and precision@k do not charge for that extra text.
  • Sections are what we picked because each chunk is one topic with a stable, citable section ID, and because the app can raise k instead of enlarging chunks if answers need more context. In the earlier equal-context run (top chunks within 4,000 characters, evals-embedding-chunking.md), sections scored recall 0.771 against 0.651 for fixed-1600. That run is outside this dashboard's two metrics and is cited here only as supporting evidence.

Caveats: 63 questions, one corpus, one run date. Precision@k is capped by how many sections a question expects (about 2–3), so it falls with k for every setup. Revisit if the corpus or question set changes.
