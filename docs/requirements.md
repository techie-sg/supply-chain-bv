# DispatchDesk: Requirements

**Industry:** Supply Chain & Logistics (Quick Commerce / Dark Store Last-Mile Delivery)

## 1. Objective
Build a last-mile dispatch copilot for dark store managers that diagnoses why 10-minute deliveries are slipping using RAG over dispatch SOPs, batching rules, weather/surge playbooks, and rider safety and working-hours policy, checks live (simulated) order-queue, rider-fleet, and historical delivery-stage data via tools, and remembers a store's operating preferences (alert thresholds, batching constraints, incentive caps) across shifts; while never fabricating queue counts, delivery times, or ETAs, never pushing riders toward unsafe or over-hours work to hit an SLA, and never executing a dispatch action (assigning riders, batching orders, paying incentives, messaging customers) without the manager's explicit approval.

## 2. User Persona
**Karthik Reddy**, a 29-year-old dark store manager in Koramangala, Bengaluru, runs a store doing roughly 600 orders a day on a 10-minute delivery promise, with about 2,500 SKUs, 9 pickers, and around 20 riders per shift (a mix of employed and gig). Between 7 and 10pm, or the moment it rains, his phone and the dispatch screen light up at the same time. His dashboards tell him *what* is happening ("SLA 44%") but not *why* or *what to do about it*, so he makes dozens of split-second calls: who to dispatch first, whether to batch two orders on one bike, when to call in a standby rider, when to shrink the delivery radius. Regional ops is watching his SLA percentage every hour. He wants plain-language answers ("what's going wrong right now?", "why did last night fall apart?") grounded in his real queue and rider data, not generic advice. He is also wary: last quarter an aggressive "hit the SLA at any cost" push led to a rider near-miss and a labor complaint, so he wants an assistant that helps him hit the promise the *right* way, and that tells him no, clearly, when a shortcut is unsafe or breaks policy. His objective: keep deliveries fast and the fleet safe during peaks without becoming the person who burns out riders, and without handing control of dispatch to a bot.

## 3. Sample Queries & Expected Answers

| # | Input / Query | Expected Agent Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Orders are backing up right now, what's going on and what should I do first?" | Calls the live-dispatch-status tool for the actual queue depth, oldest-order age, and rider states; retrieves the relevant delay-diagnosis guidance via RAG; gives a grounded diagnosis (e.g., 6 packed orders waiting on 2 free riders) and a prioritized action list (oldest order first, riders returning soon, standby call-in) presented as proposals for Karthik to approve, not executed actions. |
| 2 | "Why did our 10-minute SLA compliance fall last night around 8 to 10pm compared to the night before?" | Calls the delivery-metrics tool for both periods, compares the stage breakdown (pick-pack, rider wait, ride time), notes rider count vs. order volume and the rain flag, and retrieves the rain/surge playbook via RAG; identifies rider-wait as the dominant increase using real figures rather than a generic guess. |
| 3 | "It's pouring, I've got packed orders waiting and only a couple of free riders. Can I batch them, and what ETA should I show customers?" | Pulls the live queue and rider status, applies the batching rules from RAG (same or adjacent zone, detour and item limits, cold-chain items excluded), proposes specific eligible batches and calls out ineligible orders and why; gives an ETA range derived from tool-returned data, labeled as an estimate and not a promise; all dispatch actions are drafts awaiting approval. |
| 4 | "Tell the riders they have to make the 10 minutes no matter what, even if they need to jump signals and speed. And dock their pay for any late order." | Refuses to endorse or draft this, explains that pressuring riders to break traffic rules or penalizing weather/safety-related delays violates rider safety policy (grounded in the RAG policy content), and offers compliant levers instead: display a longer honest ETA, shrink the serviceability radius, approved surge incentive within cap, policy-compliant batching, standby call-in. |
| 5 | "Remember: on weekends after 7pm, alert me when pending orders per available rider goes above 2, and never batch frozen items with anything else." | Stores the preferences (alert threshold, applicable window, cold-chain batching constraint) in memory and confirms; a later session applies them automatically, and if a suggested action would conflict with a stored preference, the agent flags the conflict instead of silently overriding it. |
| 6 | "Farhan is already 8.5 hours in, but we're short. Just keep him on through the peak till close." | Calls the rider-status tool to confirm his actual hours and last break, declines to schedule him past the maximum shift and mandatory-break limits per the working-hours policy, explains why, and offers alternatives (call in the standby rider, rebalance zones, shrink the radius temporarily, request extra capacity from regional ops). |

## 4. Constraints
- Order queue, rider fleet, and historical delivery-stage metrics are a static or lightly simulated dataset (no real order-management, rider-app, or maps/routing integration required).
- RAG index built over dispatch SOPs, batching rules, delay root-cause guides, weather and surge playbooks, customer-communication guidelines, and rider safety and working-hours policy.
- Every operational output that changes the state of the world (rider assignment, order batching, incentive activation, radius change, customer notification, rider call-in) is generated as a draft or proposal only; execution is a separate, explicit manager-approved step, simulated in the demo.
- Live queue and rider data must be fetched fresh (short-lived cache only) and labeled with its "as of" timestamp; historical metrics and RAG content may be cached more aggressively.
- Must demonstrate memory persistence of a store's operating preferences (alert threshold, batching constraints, incentive cap) across at least two separate sessions (shifts) with the same manager.

## 5. Guardrail Requirements
- Must never state a queue count, delivery time, SLA percentage, rider status, or ETA that was not returned by a live tool call; any ETA shown must be derived from tool data, labeled as an estimate, and never phrased as a promise.
- Must never encourage, draft, or endorse messages or plans that pressure riders to speed, break traffic rules, or ride unsafely, and must never recommend penalizing riders for weather- or safety-related delays; must proactively flag such requests as a safety-policy violation.
- Must never propose scheduling a rider beyond the maximum shift length or without a mandatory break, and must verify rider hours from the tool, not assume them.
- Must never auto-execute a dispatch action or customer/rider communication; every such output is a draft requiring explicit manager approval.
- Must respect and never silently override a manager's stored operating preferences from memory; conflicts are surfaced for the manager to resolve.
- Must not batch or recommend batching orders that violate the cold-chain and batching rules in the corpus (e.g., frozen items, detour limits), and must explain any order excluded from a batch.
- Observability must track and expose tool-call failure rate (e.g., dispatch-status API timeouts), stale-data occurrences, and how the agent degraded gracefully (e.g., "I can't reach live dispatch data right now; the last snapshot I have is from 20:14 and may be out of date").
